# C1 — Matched-name randomization audit

## Closest prior work

- Di Palma et al., *Do LLMs Memorize Recommendation Datasets? A Preliminary Study on MovieLens-1M*, SIGIR 2025. [arXiv:2505.10212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.10212)
- Kim et al., *On the Memorization Behavior of LLMs in Generative Recommendation: Observations, Implications, and Training Strategies*, June 2026. [arXiv:2606.17276](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17276)
- Yu and Zhou, *Caliper: Probing Lexical Anchors versus Causal Structure in LLMs*, June 2026. [arXiv:2606.04915](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04915)
- Zhang et al., *Benchmark Leakage Trap: Can We Trust LLM-based Recommendation?*, February 2026. [arXiv:2602.13626](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.13626)
- Hou et al., *Large Language Models are Zero-Shot Rankers for Recommender Systems*, ECIR 2024. [arXiv:2305.08845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.08845)

## Findings

**F1. The proposal as written is Caliper moved to recommendation.** Caliper replaces semantic variable names with placeholder tokens. It holds the causal graph fixed. It measures the accuracy drop across nine models and three benchmarks. Your design replaces item titles with matched alternatives and holds attributes fixed. The mechanism, the perturbation, and the conclusion are the same. A reviewer who knows Caliper scores this a 3 on novelty.

**F2. The sharp null is a straw man.** The null states that recognizable names have no effect. Titles carry genre, era, tone, and franchise signal beyond any attribute list you supply. The null is false before you run the model. An exact test that rejects a null everybody knows is false is a size demonstration, not a finding.

**F3. Matching does not identify memorization.** You match on genre, popularity band, and token length. Residual semantic content survives every one of those controls. "Se7en" and "Heat" share genre, band, and length. They do not share meaning. So a rejection is consistent with generic semantic competence and needs no memorization at all.

**F4. Mismatched pairs go out of distribution.** A permutation attaches a real film title to another film's attributes. The prompt becomes internally inconsistent. A capable model can detect the inconsistency. Its behaviour under an incoherent prompt does not transfer to its behaviour under a coherent one. This breaks the link from the estimate to the deployed system.

## Repair that makes C1 publishable

Replace the single-arm permutation with a two-arm difference. Both arms perturb titles. Only one arm can touch catalogue memory.

- Arm A: permute real in-catalogue titles among matched items.
- Arm B: substitute real titles drawn from a disjoint catalogue, matched on the same covariates. Use films absent from MovieLens 1M, or products absent from Amazon Beauty.

Arm B holds fluency, world knowledge, and semantic informativeness roughly constant. Arm B removes the dataset-specific item-item co-occurrence structure that Di Palma and Kim call memorization.

## Estimand you can honestly claim

Define `tau_mem = E[D_A] - E[D_B]`, where `D` is the rank displacement of the held-out target item under the arm's perturbation. `tau_mem` is the extra displacement attributable to catalogue-specific familiarity, at fixed title fluency.

The honest claim is a validated property, not a theorem:

1. An exact finite-sample level-alpha test of the sharp null `tau_mem = 0`, valid by the design's own randomization at the list level.
2. A measured size curve from a title-blind null oracle.
3. A measured power curve from a planted-memory oracle.

Do not claim you measure memorization. Claim you measure catalogue-specific title advantage under a stated matching.

## Fatal assumptions

- **R1.** Arm B titles must be as familiar to the model as Arm A titles, on average. If Arm B titles are rarer in pretraining, `tau_mem` absorbs a familiarity gap. Mitigation: match on a pretraining-frequency proxy such as Wikipedia page views, and report the balance table.
- **R2.** The list-level randomization removes interference within a list. It does not remove position bias. Randomize position independently of title in both arms.
- **R3.** Open-weight Qwen and Llama models show much lower MovieLens coverage than GPT-4o. Di Palma et al. report 15.09% coverage for Llama-3.1 405B. Small open models may show near-zero effect. Plan for a null result and state the detectable effect size in advance.

## Smallest defensible experiment

1. Use 300 users from MovieLens 1M and 300 from Amazon Beauty.
2. Build one 20-item candidate list per user, with one held-out positive.
3. Run 30 permutations per arm per list.
4. Use Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct.
5. Score pointwise with a cached user-history prefix. This is about one million short scoring passes. One GPU handles it in a few hours.

## Strongest falsification test

Fine-tune Qwen2.5-1.5B with LoRA on a fully synthetic catalogue with known successor pairs. The test must reject on the planted model. The test must hold size on the title-blind oracle. If the test rejects on the null oracle, the design is broken and you report that.

## Scores

| Axis | As proposed | After the two-arm repair |
|---|---|---|
| Novelty | 4 | 6 |
| Technical depth | 5 | 6 |
| Feasibility | 8 | 7 |
| Venue fit | 7 | 8 |

**Verdict: conditional survivor.** The single-arm version does not deserve a paper. The two-arm difference does.

# C2 — False-discovery-controlled preference profiles

## Closest prior work

- Ramos et al., *Transparent and Scrutable Recommendations Using Natural Language User Profiles*, ACL 2024. [arXiv:2402.05810](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05810)
- *The Personalization Mirage: How LLMs Fabricate User Profiles, and Why Self-Monitoring Misleads*, August 2026. [arXiv:2608.04570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04570)
- Fostiropoulos et al., *GISTBench: Evaluating LLM User Understanding via Evidence-Based Interest Verification*, March 2026. [arXiv:2603.29112](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.29112)
- *Valid Feature-Level Inference for Tabular Foundation Models via the Conditional Randomization Test*, March 2026. [arXiv:2603.06609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.06609)
- Mohri and Hashimoto, *Language Models with Conformal Factuality Guarantees*, 2024. [arXiv:2402.10978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10978)

## Findings

**F5. GISTBench is the closest threat, and it does not close the gap.** GISTBench verifies each predicted interest against cited engagement signals. It uses an LLM judge plus hand-set count thresholds, for example two explicit positive signals. The paper states that the thresholds are domain-informed design choices. It applies no hypothesis test, no false discovery rate control, and no conformal method. It also states directly that it does not evaluate whether the predicted interests improve recommendations. Your contribution sits in exactly the space GISTBench declines to enter.

**F6. The mitigation gap is real.** The Personalization Mirage reports 35% to 49% over-inference across 12 models and 143,616 judged claims. It recommends external verification. It supplies a benchmark, not a filter with an error rate.

**F7. The conformal line does not cover you.** Mohri and Hashimoto filter claims by an entailment score against ground truth. The 2026 follow-ups control abstention and coverage. None of them define the null as counterfactual dependence on cited evidence spans. None of them run a randomization test per claim.

C2 is the only candidate whose central contribution is new rather than a routine application.

## Estimand you can honestly claim

For claim `c` with cited span set `S_c`, define the null:

`H_0c`: the test statistic `T(c)` has the same distribution when `S_c` is replaced by a control span set drawn from the within-user, same-category, sentiment-matched pool.

The honest claim is:

**Under exchangeability of the swap under the null, the Benjamini-Yekutieli procedure controls, at level alpha and under arbitrary dependence, the expected fraction of retained claims for which `H_0c` is true.**

Say the next sentence in the paper. A retained claim is a claim that depends counterfactually on the cited history. It is not a claim that is true about the user. State that distinction in the abstract. If you blur it, a reviewer kills the paper on the first read.

## Fatal assumptions

- **R4. Exchangeability is an assumption, not a fact.** A true conditional randomization test needs the conditional law of the swapped variable. You do not have it. You have a heuristic control pool. Do not call it a conditional randomization test without qualification. Cite Berrett, Wang, Barber and Samworth on the conditional permutation test, state the assumed conditional law explicitly, and report the sensitivity of the p-values to the matching radius.
- **R5. Redundant evidence produces false negatives.** A true claim supported by ten spans survives the removal of the two cited spans. The test then fails to reject and you discard a correct claim. Fix the design: swap every span above a similarity threshold, not only the cited spans. The null then becomes "no support anywhere in this user's history", which is the target you actually want.
- **R6. Benjamini-Yekutieli power is limited by p-value discreteness.** With `B` resamples the smallest attainable p-value is `1/(B+1)`. For `m = 1600` claims, `c(m) = ln(1600) + 0.577 ≈ 7.95`. If half the claims are true signals, the step-up threshold is about `800 × 0.1 / (1600 × 7.95) = 0.0063`. You need `B ≥ 159`. Use `B = 300`. If the signal fraction is low, Benjamini-Yekutieli becomes very conservative. Report the achieved power against signal fraction.
- **R7. Do not promise better held-out recommendation accuracy.** Filtering removes claims. Some removed claims carry real signal. Accuracy can fall. Promise a non-inferiority test on accuracy, plus a faithfulness improvement measured by an independent judge, plus profile-edit responsiveness. That set is defensible. A promise of accuracy improvement is not.

## Smallest defensible experiment

1. Use 200 users from Amazon Reviews 2023, one category, with at least 20 reviews each.
2. Extract about 8 atomic claims per user. That gives about 1,600 tests.
3. Run 300 swap resamples per claim. The statistic is the teacher-forced log-probability of the claim text.
4. This is about 480,000 forward passes with a shared history prefix. One GPU handles it.
5. Validate finite-sample FDR on 100 planted histories with known supported and unsupported claims. Report the realized false discovery proportion against the nominal level at alpha in {0.05, 0.1, 0.2}.

## Strongest falsification test

Run the whole pipeline on planted histories where you inserted exactly `k` unsupported claims. If the realized false discovery proportion exceeds the nominal level, the exchangeability assumption fails and you report it. Add a second falsification: shuffle the claim-to-user assignment. Every claim should then fail to reject. If claims still survive, your statistic responds to fluency rather than evidence.

# C3 — Crossed correction for circular LLM-expanded relevance judgments

## Closest prior work

- Otero and Parapar, *User Preference Induction with LLMs for Offline Top-N Recommendation Evaluation*, July 2026. [arXiv:2607.11354](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11354)
- *Eval-Pair Matrix: Answer-Paired Meta-Evaluation of LLM Judges for Grounded RAG*, July 2026. [arXiv:2607.10626](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.10626)
- Xu, Zeng, Paisley and Zhao, *When Can You Debias an LLM Judge? Identifiability Limits, a Test, and Designs for Top-k Ranking*, July 2026. [arXiv:2607.02104](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.02104)
- Darshan and Divekar, *Statistically Reliable LLM-Based Ranking Evaluation via Prediction-Powered Inference*, June 2026. [arXiv:2606.05308](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05308), and *PRECISE*, AAAI 2026. [arXiv:2601.18777](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.18777)
- Dietz et al., *Principles and Guidelines for the Use of LLM Judges*, 2025. [PDF](https://www.cs.unh.edu/~dietz/papers/dietz2025principles.pdf)

## Why this one dies

**F8. The design is already published, one domain over.** Eval-Pair Matrix runs a crossed 3x3 generator-by-judge matrix. It estimates same-model effects by pairing judges on the exact same candidate answer. It reports 300 core records, 897 generator outputs, and 2,683 judge verdicts. Your "answer/item-paired contrasts while controlling main effects" is the same estimator. Moving it from grounded RAG to recommendation is a domain transfer, not a method.

**F9. The prescription already exists.** Dietz et al. 2025 already tell practitioners to use two different LLMs for generation and evaluation. Clarke and Dietz already demonstrated the effect: a run built with the judge model ranked 5th under LLM evaluation and 28th under manual evaluation. Your paper would confirm advice that is already standard.

**F10. The correction is not identified.** Xu et al. prove that the item-level bias coefficient is non-identified from comparisons alone. Their Proposition 2 shows the likelihood is invariant under `theta_i -> theta_i + delta·x_i` and `c -> c - delta`. They measure a flat profile likelihood, 0.0000 nats, across 48 real judge-pools, even after they scale the comparison count 26 times. Your calibrated bias correction is a prior assumption dressed as a measurement, unless you supply gold labels.

**F11. The gold labels you plan to use are the wrong labels.** You calibrate on deliberately masked observed ratings. You then apply the correction to genuinely unjudged pooled items. Observed ratings are missing-not-at-random. They concentrate on popular and exposed items. Unjudged items are exactly the non-exposed ones. The calibration population and the target population differ on the one variable that drives the bias. This is a transportability failure and no amount of bootstrapping repairs it.

**F12. The correct method for this task already exists and is stronger.** Prediction-powered inference gives provably unbiased ranking-metric estimates from a small labeled set plus a large LLM-judged set. PRECISE is at AAAI 2026 and handles Precision@K and nDCG-style metrics. Your ad hoc calibration is a weaker version of a published, guaranteed method.

**Kill it.** Novelty 3, technical depth 5, feasibility 5, venue fit 6. It fails the 6/6 bar on novelty and it fails on identification.

The one honest sentence you can keep from C3: Otero and Parapar use `nvidia/Llama-4-Scout-17B-16E-Instruct-FP4` for both the induction stage and the judge stage. They write that circularity effects are future work. That observation belongs in the related work section of C4, not in a paper of its own.

# C4 — Replacement: exposure-aware sensitivity bounds for LLM-completed offline evaluation

I ground this in three explicit limitations of named papers.

1. Otero and Parapar list "adaptive completion policies that explicitly control popularity exposure" and "uncertainty-aware judging pipelines" as future work.
2. Song et al., *Demystifying Prediction Powered Inference*, January 2026, state that under missing-not-at-random mechanisms all methods, including classical inference on labeled data alone, yield biased estimates. [arXiv:2601.20819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.20819)
3. PRECISE assumes the labeled subset is a random sample of the target population. In offline recommendation the labeled subset is the exposed subset. The assumption fails by construction.

## The idea

Do not correct the bias. Bound the conclusion.

Setting: two recommenders A and B produce top-10 lists. Observed ratings label a small exposed subset. An LLM judge labels every pooled item. Target: `Delta = nDCG@10(A) - nDCG@10(B)` under the complete relevance function.

Stratify pooled items by exposure propensity, using popularity decile as the proxy. Estimate per-stratum judge false-positive and false-negative rates on the masked observed ratings. Do not transport those rates. Treat them as the lower end of a plausible range and let the rate grow with the exposure gap.

Then compute the sharp interval for `Delta` over every relevance assignment consistent with the per-stratum error budgets. Report the **flip point**: the smallest per-stratum judge error rate at which the sign of `Delta` changes.

## What you can prove

nDCG@10 is a weighted sum of per-position gains with fixed positive discounts. Under a per-stratum budget on the number of flipped judgments, the extremum of `Delta` is attained by a greedy exchange. Flip the judgments with the largest discount weight first, subject to the budget.

**Claimable theorem.** Given per-stratum flip budgets, the greedy assignment attains the exact maximum and the exact minimum of `Delta`. Therefore the reported interval is sharp, not conservative.

The proof is a standard exchange argument. It fits in half a page. A strong high-school student can write it and verify it by brute force over all assignments on 5-item lists.

## Validation

Take dense MovieLens 32M users where most items are rated. Treat their full rating vectors as complete relevance. Simulate exposure by popularity-biased masking. Check that the interval covers the true `Delta` at the stated budgets. Check that a naive prediction-powered estimate fitted on the masked subset falls outside the interval when the exposure bias is strong. That contrast is the paper's headline figure.

## Falsification

Set the judge error budget to zero. The interval must collapse to the point estimate under the LLM judgments. Set the budget to one. The interval must span every attainable `Delta`. If either check fails, the bound is wrong.

The honest risk: the interval may be so wide that every recommender comparison is inconclusive. That is still a result, and it is the result the field needs. State the risk in the introduction so no reviewer treats it as a surprise.

## Feasibility

The bound needs no GPU. The GPU produces the judgments once. You can also reuse the released judgments from Otero and Parapar if they published them. This is the most feasible of the four candidates before 20 September.

Novelty 6. Technical depth 7. Feasibility 7. Venue fit 8.
