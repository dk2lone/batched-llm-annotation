## Recap

We are at round 4 of the adversarial review for the IEEE BigData 2026 High School Symposium. Round 3 produced P1, P2 and P3. Round 3 already killed P3 on a direct collision. This round decides P1 and P2, then delivers three survivors.

Result: P1 and P2 both die. Three replacements pass the bar. All three come from independent searches, not from the candidate descriptions.

---

## Kills

### P1 — difference in discontinuities at a model cutoff. KILLED.

**K1. Direct method collision.** Zhang and Stadie already propose both halves of P1. Their route 1 is a regression discontinuity at a known cutoff. Their route 2 is a difference in differences against a clean control model. P1 is the composition of these two routes, moved to recommendation.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02985

**K2. Identification fails, and the failure is already proved.** The same paper proves that leakage is not identified from passive scores. Recency and leakage share the same threshold. Their identified set for inflation does not shrink as data grows. A flat or a sharp profile at the cutoff proves nothing on its own.

**K3. The threshold is not a real threshold.** Effective cutoffs diverge from reported cutoffs. One measured case shows a 2023 reported date with knowledge concentrated near 2019. A running variable measured against a wrong threshold produces a biased jump.
https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12958

**K4. The estimand is weak, as you suspected.** Release before a cutoff gives eligibility only. The jump is an intent-to-treat effect on an unobserved treatment. No first stage measures actual exposure, so no scale-up to a treatment effect exists.

**K5. No dataset supports it.** Amazon Reviews 2023 ends in 2023. It covers only old cutoffs. No public recommendation corpus carries fine item release dates plus dense interactions on both sides of 2024 and 2025 cutoffs.

**K6. The control does not control.** A collaborative baseline and an LLM respond differently to catalog changes and interface changes. A platform discontinuity does not cancel in the difference.

P1 is dead on four independent grounds. Do not repair it.

### P2 — exact history-identity test and lower bound on personalized users. KILLED.

**K7. The p-value is unnecessary.** With greedy scoring, every counterfactual is computable. The invariance null fixes all outcomes. The per-user randomization distribution is degenerate. No randomness supports inference.

**K8. The alternative null is invalid.** State the null instead as "the own history gives no advantage". Donor histories are then not exchangeable with the own history. The held-out item came from the same user process that produced the own history. Matching on length, activity, category composition and time does not remove this. Within-category item-level correlation survives every match.

**K9. So closed testing has nothing valid to close over.** Vesely, Finos and Goeman guarantee a true-discovery lower bound only for valid local tests. Invalid per-user tests give an invalid bound.

**K10. Novelty was never the problem.** Three query formulations found no paper that runs per-user history-identity tests with multiplicity control or true-discovery lower bounds. The nearest work replaces single profile slots and reports no p-values (https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08180). The nearest counterfactual-probe work swaps protected attributes, not history identity (https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08284). The object is simply not well posed.

## S1 — Memorization breaks the Type-I error guarantee of agentic hypothesis validation

**Topic 7 and topic 2.**

### 1. Question and contribution

Popper gives strict Type-I error control for LLM agents that propose a hypothesis and then falsify it on data. The guarantee needs one condition: the proposal must not depend on the validation data except through what the agent saw. Public tabular datasets sit inside pretraining corpora. The condition therefore fails by construction on exactly the datasets everyone uses.

Four contributions:

1. State the measurability condition that sample splitting, data thinning and sequential falsification all rely on. Show that memorization routes the dependence through model weights, not through data.
2. Bound the Type-I inflation by one measurable quantity: the probability that the agent asserts the memorized association when the supplied data contradicts it.
3. Give an exactly valid calibration measurement. Permute the response column against the feature rows. The features keep their joint structure. The null holds by construction. Run the whole pipeline. The rejection rate is an unbiased estimate of the real Type-I error. This stays valid under memorization, because truth is fixed by design, not by assumption.
4. Give a repair and measure its cost. Re-key the dataset: rename columns and categories to random tokens, apply a monotone rescale to numeric columns. Trace the validity-utility frontier of Type-I error against power, as a function of re-key strength.

**Estimand:** the real Type-I error rate at nominal alpha, for memorized datasets against fresh datasets, plus power at fixed Type-I error along the re-key path.

### 2. Closest papers

- Popper, agentic sequential falsification with Type-I error control: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09858
- Probing Memorization of Tabular In-Context Learning: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31208
- Generalized Data Thinning Using Sufficient Statistics: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12931
- Inference conditional on selection, a review: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.09779
- Hypothesis Generation with Large Language Models: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.04326

### 3. Collision check

No. Popper states the guarantee and assumes the condition. The tabular memorization work measures memorization but never connects it to test validity. No paper measures Type-I inflation caused by pretraining memorization, and no paper traces a re-key frontier.

### 4. Validity audit

- The permuted-null calibration is exactly valid. You control the randomization. Permute the response only, never all columns.
- The bound in contribution 2 is the weak part. It is a union-type bound, not an identification result. Say so in the paper.
- The re-key repair is a heuristic. It can fail when the pattern is recoverable from marginal statistics alone.

### 5. Smallest decisive experiment

1. Select 8 datasets certainly present in pretraining data: Adult, Titanic, Wine, German Credit, Heart Disease, Diabetes, Iris, Abalone.
2. Select 5 fresh datasets published after 2025, or simulate them.
3. Build 200 response-permuted replicates per dataset.
4. Run the pipeline at alpha equal to 0.1. Record the rejection rate.
5. Repeat step 4 at 4 re-key levels.

Compute: one open 8B model served with vLLM on one GPU. About 100k calls total. Estimate 12 to 30 GPU hours.

**Strongest falsification:** memorized and fresh datasets give the same rejection rate at nominal alpha. The concern is then empty.

### 6. Most likely fatal assumption

That memorization is strong enough to override contradicting in-context evidence. If in-context data always dominates the prior, inflation reads near zero.

### 7. Scores

Novelty 7. Technical depth 7. Feasibility 8. Venue fit 8.

## S2 — Budget-optimal design for full-catalog ranking metrics

**Topic 2 and topic 5.**

### 1. Question and contribution

An LLM scorer costs one forward pass per item. Catalogs hold 10^5 to 10^6 items. Papers therefore score a sampled candidate set. Krichene and Rendle proved that uniform sampled metrics are inconsistent, and gave bias corrections. One question stays open. At a fixed budget of m scores per user, which sampling design minimizes the error of the full-catalog metric, and what error is unavoidable?

Four contributions:

1. A query-complexity lower bound. Any deterministic method that certifies the exact rank of the target item needs Omega(|C|) scores in the worst case. Every sublinear evaluation must therefore be statistical. The adversary argument is short and provable.
2. Treat the rank R = 1 + sum over j of 1{s_j > s_t} as a finite-population total of a binary variable. A Horvitz-Thompson estimator with proposal q is unbiased for R. Derive the variance-minimizing q. It puts mass near s_t, because only items near the boundary carry variance. This is a Neyman allocation, not a heuristic.
3. A two-stage estimator. Stage 1 uses a cheap collaborative model to predict boundary items. Stage 2 samples from that proposal and applies the correction. Prove unbiasedness for any full-support proposal, and give the variance in terms of proposal calibration.
4. Report a confidence interval for R, then map it through the monotone metric function. The interval stays exactly valid, because monotone maps preserve interval coverage.

**Estimand:** exact full-catalog Recall@k and NDCG@k of the LLM ranker.

### 2. Closest papers

- Krichene and Rendle, On Sampled Metrics for Item Recommendation: https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3394486.3403226
- Ihemelandu and Ekstrand, Candidate Set Sampling for Evaluating Top-N Recommendation: https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.11723
- On Item-Sampling Evaluation for Recommender System: https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3629171
- Improved Estimation of Ranks for Learning Item Recommenders with Negative Sampling: https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.06371
- Davy, Clemencon and Laclau, Doing well with less: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02345

### 3. Collision check

Partial overlap on the estimand only. Every prior work assumes uniform item sampling and corrects the bias afterwards. Ihemelandu and Ekstrand run a simulation study. Davy and co-authors target pairwise empirical risk, not ranking metrics. None derives the variance-optimal proposal. None gives a query-complexity lower bound. None targets the per-user forward-pass budget of an LLM scorer.

### 4. Validity audit

- Unbiasedness needs a proposal with positive mass on every item, and known inclusion probabilities. Both hold, because you choose the proposal.
- The target score s_t must be known. That costs one extra call per user.
- NDCG@k is a nonlinear function of R. An unbiased estimate of R does not give an unbiased estimate of the metric. Contribution 4 handles this with a monotone interval map. State the point openly, because a reviewer will look for it.
- The optimal proposal depends on unknown boundary probabilities. The two-stage plug-in is not exactly optimal. Report the loss under misspecification.

### 5. Smallest decisive experiment

1. Take one Amazon Reviews 2023 category. Restrict to a catalog of about 20,000 items and 500 test users.
2. Score the full catalog once with prefix-tree batched scoring. This is the ground truth.
3. Compare uniform sampling plus the Krichene-Rendle correction against the two-stage design, at m equal to 20, 50, 100 and 200.
4. Report mean squared error, interval width and realized coverage.

Compute: one 1B to 2B open model on one GPU. Estimate 10 to 20 GPU hours, dominated by step 2.

**Strongest falsification:** at equal m, the two-stage design does not reduce mean squared error against uniform sampling plus correction.

### 6. Most likely fatal assumption

That a cheap collaborative model can localize the boundary. If the LLM score order is close to independent of collaborative scores, the proposal carries no information, and the design collapses to uniform.

### 7. Scores

Novelty 6. Technical depth 7. Feasibility 7. Venue fit 9.

## S3 — Randomized pretraining exposure, split into content and co-occurrence

**Topic 5 and topic 7.**

### 1. Question and contribution

LLM recommenders beat collaborative baselines on some benchmarks. Two explanations compete. First, the model knows what the items are. Second, web text already encodes which items go together, so the model receives collaborative signal for free. This candidate answers the original P1 question with a design that identifies the effect, instead of a cutoff that does not.

Five contributions:

1. Take a held-out item set. Assign each item at random to one of four arms: no document, content-only document, co-occurrence-only document, both. You control the assignment, so both effects are identified by design. No cutoff, no eligibility proxy, no unmeasured exposure.
2. Add a dose factor of 1, 4 or 16 documents per item. This gives a dose-response curve.
3. Continue pretraining one small open base model on this corpus. Carry all arms in one run with the Bordt and Pawelczyk single-run design. Run their dependence test for arm interaction.
4. Fine-tune every arm on identical recommendation data. Any difference in per-item ranking success then comes from the injected pretraining text alone.
5. Report the average causal effect of content exposure, the average causal effect of co-occurrence exposure, and the interaction.

**Estimand:** the item-level average causal effect of each exposure type on per-item ranking success, plus the dose-response curve.

### 2. Closest papers

- Kim et al., On the Memorization Behavior of LLMs in Generative Recommendation: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17276
- Mozafari, Piryani and Jatowt, Pretraining Exposure Explains Popularity Judgments in LLMs, SIGIR 2026: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12382
- Bordt and Pawelczyk, Train Once, Answer All, ICLR 2026: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23383
- Zhang and Stadie, Temporal Leakage in LLM Backtesting: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02985
- Entity Frequency in Pre-training Induces Asymmetry in LLMs: https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.22362
- Synthetic continued pretraining: https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.07431

### 3. Collision check

No. The memorization paper is observational, and studies transitions memorized during fine-tuning, not pretraining exposure. The SIGIR paper correlates measured exposure with popularity judgments for entities, and never touches ranking success or randomization. Zhang and Stadie inject leakage into twin models, but for forecast backtests, and only to validate an estimator. No paper randomizes item-level pretraining exposure with a recommendation outcome, and no paper separates content from co-occurrence.

### 4. Validity audit

- Randomization identifies the average causal effect without assumptions. This is the strongest identification of the three candidates.
- The randomized unit is the item. Hundreds of items per arm give valid item-level randomization inference from one training run.
- Threat: the base model may already know these items. The estimate is then a marginal effect at an unknown baseline, not the effect of first exposure. Fix by selecting items absent from the base corpus, or by using synthetic item names. Synthetic names weaken external validity. State the trade-off.
- Threat: injected documents are synthetic. The estimate applies to that text distribution.
- Threat: the single-run design assumes no interaction between arms. Run the published dependence test and report it.

### 5. Smallest decisive experiment

1. Choose OLMo-2-1B or Qwen3-1.7B base.
2. Select 800 held-out items from one Amazon Reviews 2023 category. Assign 200 items per arm.
3. Build a corpus of about 500M general tokens plus 20M to 60M tokens of injected item documents.
4. Continue pretraining on one H100. Estimate 6 to 15 GPU hours.
5. Probe closed-book recall of the injected facts. If the probe fails, stop. The run learned nothing, and the recommendation result would be uninformative.
6. Fine-tune every arm identically with LoRA. Estimate 2 GPU hours.
7. Measure per-item hit at 10. Run the item-level randomization test per arm contrast. Plot the dose-response.

**Strongest falsification:** both arms read zero at every dose, while step 5 confirms the model learned the documents. Pretraining exposure then does not explain the LLM advantage.

### 6. Most likely fatal assumption

That a 1B model continued on 500M tokens absorbs the injected documents well enough to change ranking behavior. Step 5 exists to catch this before the expensive stage.

### 7. Scores

Novelty 7. Technical depth 7. Feasibility 6. Venue fit 8.

## Recommendation and next action

Start with **S1**. It has the highest feasibility, needs no pretraining run, and attacks a stated assumption in a strong 2025 statistics paper rather than a vague gap. The whole result fits in 5 pages: one condition, one bound, one calibration measurement, one frontier plot. S2 is the safe second choice if the student prefers pure statistics over LLM work. S3 is the highest-risk choice, because a weak continued-pretraining run produces an uninformative null.

One action waits on the student: pick S1, S2 or S3, then run the smallest decisive experiment for that candidate first, before any writing.
